# The Horseshoe District: Revenue and Economic Impact Analysis

**Status: PRELIMINARY working model. Every figure below is an order-of-magnitude
estimate built from published comparables, intended to frame the opportunity.
All numbers require validation by a formal feasibility study (e.g., HVS, CSL,
Hunden Partners) before being represented to investors or public bodies.**

This document is the methodology appendix for the figures shown on the pitch
site. The site stays clean; the math and sources live here.

## 2. Athletics / university revenue model, by precinct (annual, stabilized)

### 2.1 The Amphitheater: ~$3M to $5M

| Stream | Math | Estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ticketing share (rent + facility fees) | 55 events x 3,000 avg attendance x ~$10/ticket venue share | ~$1.7M |
| Food and beverage margin | 165K attendees x $18 per cap x ~35% venue margin | ~$1.0M |
| Venue naming rights | College venue deals run $1.0M to $1.85M/yr (Kroger Field: $1.85M/yr); a 4,300-seat amphitheater prices below a stadium | ~$0.75M |
| Sponsorship / premium boxes | Placeholder | ~$0.5M |

Benchmark: a feasibility study for a comparable 5,000-seat amphitheater
projected ~76 ticketed events and >$4.4M in shareable ticket revenue by year
five (Brackenridge Park / SGT feasibility study). We assume fewer events (55)
to stay conservative.

### 2.2 The Scarlet Spine: ~$6M to $7M

| Stream | Math | Estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retail / restaurant leases | 150,000 SF x ~$35/SF NNN | ~$5.3M |
| Flagship team store merch uplift | OSU royalties/licensing/sponsorship is an 8-figure annual category (FY24 NCAA report); a flagship district store adds incremental licensed sales | ~$1.0M |
| Sponsorship activation zones | Game-day brand activations | ~$0.5M |

### 2.3 Buckeye Tower (hotel): ~$1.5M to $2M direct, plus naming partner upside

| Stream | Math | Estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rooms revenue (hotel level) | 200 keys x $185 ADR x 68% occupancy | ~$9.2M |
| Total hotel revenue incl. F&B/conference | ~1.5x rooms revenue | ~$13.8M |
| University ground lease + participation | ~12% effective participation | ~$1.6M |

ADR assumption is a premium to the Columbus market (market ADR has run in the
low-$100s to ~$150s with strong post-pandemic growth per HVS market
commentary), justified by stadium adjacency and event compression nights.

### 2.4 Residential Towers: ~$1M to $1.5M

| Stream | Math | Estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross rents (developer level) | 600 units x $1,850/mo x 95% occupancy | ~$12.6M |
| University ground lease (effective ~10%) | Endowment-style income | ~$1.3M |

### 2.5 The Riverfront: ~$0.25M plus catalytic value

Trail/park naming and sponsorship. The riverfront's main role is raising land
values and dwell time for the other four precincts (see Olentangy Trail and
Rails-to-Trails case material for trail-adjacent value effects).

### 2.6 District-wide streams (not precinct-specific): ~$4M to $7M

| Stream | Benchmark | Estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| District naming rights | Kroger Field: $1.85M/yr for one venue; OSU brand reach supports a premium for an entire district | $2M to $3M |
| District sponsorship inventory | Pouring rights, banking, telecom, etc. | $1M to $2M |
| Premium game-day hospitality | 100K+ visitors, 7+ home games | $1M to $2M |

### 2.7 Total athletics / university-linked revenue (stabilized)

**Roughly $15M to $22M per year.** For context, Ohio State athletics reported
a record ~$336M of revenue in FY25 (Ohio State News) and $58.8M of ticket
revenue in FY24 (NCAA report via Yahoo/Dayton Daily News), so the district
would add a mid-single-digit percentage of durable, non-media revenue, which
is exactly the diversification athletic departments are seeking post-House
settlement.

## 3. Civic and economic impact (city / state funding case)

### 3.1 One-time construction impact

- Spend: ~$800M placeholder.
- Output multiplier: BEA RIMS II-style construction multipliers run roughly
  1.8x to 3.0x total output, i.e. **$1.4B to $2.4B** in one-time economic
  output.
- Employment: published RIMS II-derived figures range from ~5.5 to 8+
  direct-plus-indirect job-years per $1M of construction spend (higher when
  induced effects are included), i.e. **~4,500 to 6,500 construction
  job-years** over the build-out.

### 3.2 Ongoing annual impact (stabilized)

| Metric | Estimate | Benchmarks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual regional economic activity | **$100M to $175M** | Columbus Arena District: ~$150M/yr to Franklin County after 25 years (FCCFA, 2026); Titletown: $50M to $70M/yr on 45 acres |
| Permanent jobs | **1,200 to 1,800** | Titletown: ~1,000 jobs; Nationwide Arena: ~1,000 direct jobs |
| Annual state and local tax revenue | **$15M to $30M** | Truist Park + The Battery: $38M (2022) across county, state, schools; Cobb fiscal study: $18.9M/yr average net fiscal impact |
| Overnight visitors | meaningful hotel-night generation | Nationwide Arena: ~350K overnight visitors/yr |

### 3.3 Property value creation

The Battery Atlanta's taxable property value grew from $5M (2014) to $736M
(2022) (Urbanize Atlanta). A similar trajectory on currently low-value land
beside Ohio Stadium is the core of the public-funding argument: the district
pays for its own services through value it creates.

## 5. Caveats

1. Every operating figure is a placeholder pending a commissioned feasibility
   study with real program sizing, market studies, and deal structures.
2. Comparables are directional, not transferable: The Battery and Titletown
   are pro-team districts with different control and calendar dynamics.
3. Athletics revenue capture depends entirely on deal structure (who owns,
   who operates, who holds naming/sponsorship inventory). The ranges assume
   the university/athletics retains ground ownership and sponsorship rights.
4. Fiscal impact studies commissioned by interested parties (e.g., team or
   chamber-commissioned studies) tend to be optimistic; the independent
   academic literature on stadium subsidies is more skeptical. Use the
   conservative end of ranges in public-funding conversations.
